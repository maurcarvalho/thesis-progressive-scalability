\label{sec:conclusion}

This chapter completes the research proposal by summarizing the contributions developed so far, outlining the next stages of investigation, and articulating the boundaries that shape this work. The previous chapters established the empirical and conceptual foundation for examining modular monoliths as a viable architectural choice for early-stage cloud-native systems. Building on that foundation, the chapter that closes this dissertation revisits the guidelines proposed in Chapter~\ref{sec:proposal}, positions them within the broader research agenda, and identifies the path toward field validation.

\section{Summary of Contributions}

This research proposes a set of architectural guidelines for software startups adopting modular monolith architectures while preserving scalability, maintainability, and internal modularity. Of the twelve guidelines originally synthesized from the systematic literature review, six are fully developed in this dissertation and cover the Architectural Design and Operational Fit dimensions. Together, they form a progression in which each guideline builds on the previous one, moving from enforced modularity toward observable, extraction-ready architecture.

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=8pt,topsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{G1, Enforce Modular Boundaries:} positions modularity as an enforceable property, sustained through explicit declarations, automated verification, and build-time checks.
  \item \textbf{G2, Embed Maintainability:} treats maintainability as a bounded cost of change, tracked through stable contracts, unidirectional dependencies, and structural metrics.
  \item \textbf{G3, Design for Progressive Scalability:} introduces a four-level scalability spectrum (L0 to L3) that absorbs growth through proportional, evidence-driven interventions.
  \item \textbf{G4, Promote Migration Readiness:} prepares modules for extraction within the monolith through event-streaming and saga orchestration patterns implemented with Apache Kafka and Temporal.
  \item \textbf{G5, Streamline Deployment Strategy:} defines a deployment spectrum (D0 to D2) that mirrors G3, supported by a single monorepo and module-aware CI/CD pipelines.
  \item \textbf{G6, Introduce Observability Patterns:} establishes module-scoped observability with OpenTelemetry, Prometheus, Grafana, and Jaeger that survives extraction unchanged.
\end{itemize}

The guidelines are grounded in Tiny Store, a working reference implementation built as an Nx monorepo with four bounded contexts. All code listings in G1 to G6 are drawn from repository source files and verified against the running system, ensuring that each principle is operationalized rather than purely conceptual.

The systematic literature review revealed that existing studies cover individual techniques such as modular boundary enforcement, maintainability patterns, migration strategies, deployment automation, and observability tooling, yet none of the eighteen reviewed articles integrate these techniques into a cohesive, progressive guideline set. The contribution of this work lies precisely in that integration: the G1 to G6 progression synthesizes individually studied techniques into a deliberate scalability path. Schema isolation, caching, asynchronous queues, event-driven communication, and distributed tracing are positioned as architectural requirements from project inception, not as migration prerequisites added later, so that extraction becomes a deployment-topology change rather than a codebase rewrite.

\section{Next Steps}

The next stage of this research is to execute the verification plan outlined in Chapter~\ref{sec:proposal}. Semi-structured interviews with industry practitioners and academic researchers will confirm, refine, or extend the guidelines and surface additional trade-offs that emerge in practice. Insights from these interviews, combined with targeted research in gray literature, will inform iterative revisions toward more actionable, context-aware heuristics. Through this reflective cycle, the guidelines will transition from literature-derived hypotheses to field-validated tools, ensuring clarity, applicability, and relevance in real-world startup environments.

The six deferred guidelines (G7 to G12), covering the Organizational Alignment and Guideline Orientation dimensions, will be developed in a subsequent phase. Their treatment requires methods beyond code-level analysis, including team surveys, onboarding measurements, and organizational case studies, and they will be validated through the same interview-based methodology applied to G1 to G6.

A natural extension of this work, and arguably the most effective path to practical evaluation, is to package the guidelines as developer tooling. The most direct route is to extend mainstream application frameworks with first-class support for modular monolith design: a Ruby on Rails extension that treats modules as native architectural units, with built-in boundary enforcement, module-scoped configuration, and extraction-ready conventions, would expose the guidelines to a large practitioner community while preserving the ergonomics that make Rails the dominant choice for early-stage startups. Beyond framework integration, language-specific libraries and AI coding assistants or agents are a viable complementary path, surfacing boundary, scalability, and extraction-readiness checks at development time. Together, these directions would lower adoption barriers and turn progressive scalability into a reproducible engineering practice rather than an architectural discipline that depends on team maturity alone.

\section{Scope and Limitations}

The research deliberately focuses on early-stage, cloud-native software startups, contexts in which architectural decisions are often made under uncertainty, with limited time, infrastructure, and people. This scope excludes large-scale enterprise systems, highly regulated industries, and long-established software organizations, where architectural dynamics may differ substantially. The proposed guidelines are framed as decision-making heuristics rather than prescriptive rules, and the current work is bounded to the Architectural Design and Operational Fit dimensions; Organizational Alignment and Guideline Orientation are identified as future research.

Several additional limitations must be acknowledged. First, the reference implementation relies on a specific technology stack that includes Apache Kafka, Temporal, OpenTelemetry, and Kamal. Although the underlying patterns are transferable to alternatives such as RabbitMQ, AWS Step Functions, Datadog, and Kubernetes, validation with alternative stacks remains a necessary extension. Second, Tiny Store is deliberately small in scope and does not capture the full complexity of a production system, which makes validation against larger, real-world codebases an important next step. Third, the availability and diversity of expert participants for the verification phase may constrain generalizability, a risk that will be mitigated through transparent methodology, critical triangulation, and clear scope delimitation.

\section{Final Remarks}

This dissertation positions architectural guidance not as a static artifact, but as an evolving construct shaped by both empirical evidence and contextual complexity. The central argument of this work, that modular monoliths can achieve progressive scalability through deliberate, evidence-driven architectural interventions without premature distribution, is operationalized through six guidelines and a working reference implementation. The next phase of this research moves beyond synthesis into engagement, testing whether these principles can meaningfully support the design and evolution of systems that remain simple in structure, scalable in function, and sustainable in team practice. The most promising route to that evaluation is to embed the guidelines into the tools developers already use every day: extending established application frameworks such as Ruby on Rails to natively support modular monolith applications, complemented by language-specific libraries and AI coding assistants that operationalize boundary, scalability, and extraction-readiness checks at the moment code is written. By meeting practitioners inside their existing workflows, progressive scalability stops being an architectural intention and becomes a default of the development environment itself.
