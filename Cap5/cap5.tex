\label{sec:conclusion}

This chapter summarizes the contributions developed so far, outlines the next research stages, and defines the boundaries that shape this work.

\section{Summary of Contributions}

This research proposes architectural guidelines for software startups adopting modular monolith architectures that preserve scalability, maintainability, and internal modularity. Six of the twelve originally proposed guidelines (G1--G6) are fully developed in this dissertation, covering the Architectural Design and Operational Fit dimensions:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{G1: Enforce Modular Boundaries} — modularity as an enforceable property through explicit declarations, automated verification, and build-time checks.
  \item \textbf{G2: Embed Maintainability} — maintainability as a bounded cost of change, tracked through stable contracts, unidirectional dependencies, and structural metrics.
  \item \textbf{G3: Design for Progressive Scalability} — a four-level scalability spectrum (L0--L3) that absorbs growth through proportional, evidence-driven interventions.
  \item \textbf{G4: Promote Migration Readiness} — modules made extraction-ready inside the monolith using Apache Kafka and Temporal.
  \item \textbf{G5: Streamline Deployment Strategy} — a deployment spectrum (D0--D2) that mirrors G3, with a single monorepo and Nx-based module-aware CI/CD.
  \item \textbf{G6: Introduce Observability Patterns} — module-scoped observability with OpenTelemetry, Prometheus, Grafana, and Jaeger that survives extraction unchanged.
\end{itemize}

The guidelines are grounded in Tiny Store, a working reference implementation built as an Nx monorepo with four bounded contexts. All code listings in G3--G6 are drawn from repository source files and verified against the running system.

The systematic literature review found that existing studies cover individual techniques (modular boundaries, maintainability, migration, deployment, observability) in isolation, but none integrate them into a cohesive, progressive guideline set. The contribution of this work is that integration: the G1$\rightarrow$G6 progression synthesizes individually studied techniques into a deliberate scalability path. Schema isolation, caching, async queues, event-driven communication, and distributed tracing are positioned as architectural requirements from project inception, not migration prerequisites added later, so that extraction becomes a deployment-topology change rather than a codebase rewrite.

\section{Next Steps}

The next step is to conduct the verification plan outlined in Chapter~\ref{sec:proposal}. Semi-structured interviews with industry practitioners and academic researchers will confirm, refine, or extend the guidelines and surface additional trade-offs. Insights from these interviews, combined with targeted research in gray literature, will inform revisions toward more actionable, context-aware heuristics.

The six deferred guidelines (G7--G12) covering Organizational Alignment and Guideline Orientation will be developed in a subsequent phase. They require methods beyond code-level analysis, including team surveys, onboarding measurements, and organizational case studies, and will be validated through the same interview-based methodology used for G1--G6.

A future direction is to package the guidelines as developer tooling, for example as language-specific libraries or a coding-assistant integration that surfaces boundary, scalability, and extraction-readiness checks at development time. This would lower adoption barriers and make progressive scalability a reproducible engineering practice.

\section{Scope and Limitations}

The research focuses on early-stage, cloud-native software startups, where architectural decisions are made under uncertainty with limited time, infrastructure, and people. This scope excludes large-scale enterprise systems, highly regulated industries, and long-established organizations, where architectural dynamics may differ substantially.

The guidelines are framed as decision-making heuristics, not prescriptive rules. The current work is limited to the Architectural Design and Operational Fit dimensions; Organizational Alignment and Guideline Orientation are identified as future research.

Additional limitations:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=4pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item The implementation uses a specific stack (Kafka, Temporal, OpenTelemetry, Kamal). The underlying patterns are transferable to alternatives (RabbitMQ, AWS Step Functions, Datadog, Kubernetes), but should be validated with alternative stacks in future work.
  \item Tiny Store is deliberately small and does not capture the full complexity of a production system. Validation with larger, real-world codebases is a necessary extension.
  \item The availability and diversity of expert participants for verification may constrain generalizability, mitigated through transparent methodology and clear scope delimitation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Final Remarks}

The central argument of this work, that modular monoliths can achieve progressive scalability through deliberate, evidence-driven architectural interventions without premature distribution, is operationalized through six guidelines and a working reference implementation. The next phase moves from synthesis into engagement: testing whether these principles meaningfully support the design and evolution of systems that remain simple in structure, scalable in function, and sustainable in team practice.
