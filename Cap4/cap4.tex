\label{sec:proposal}

This chapter presents the core contribution of this research: a set of architectural guidelines for software startups adopting modular monolithic architectures. The guidelines are grounded in the gaps and trade-offs identified through the systematic literature review and reflect the architectural tensions of early-stage teams operating under limited resources, rapid iteration, and evolving requirements. They are framed as decision-making heuristics rather than a rigid prescriptive framework.

The research design builds on the twelve evaluation criteria identified through the systematic literature review, organized across four analytical dimensions introduced in Chapter~\ref{sec:relatedwork}: Architectural Design, Operational Fit, Organizational Alignment, and Guideline Orientation. This dissertation proposes six guidelines (G1--G6) that operationalize the first two dimensions, covering module boundaries, maintainability, progressive scalability, migration readiness, deployment strategy, and observability. The remaining two dimensions, Organizational Alignment and Guideline Orientation, are framed as future research (Section~\ref{sec:future-guidelines}).

\noindent
Figure~\ref{fig:guideline-dimensions} shows the four guideline dimensions used in this research to guide the evaluation of modular monolith architectures in startup environments:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=8pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Architectural Design.} Covers criteria related to the software's internal structure. Guideline G1 enforces clear module boundaries, G2 evaluates maintainability over time, and G3 designs for progressive scalability.

  \item \textbf{Operational Fit.} Addresses the operational requirements needed to support production environments. Guideline G4 checks migration readiness (for example, transitioning toward microservices), G5 focuses on deployment strategy and automation, and G6 considers observability levels (logging, metrics, monitoring).

  \item \textbf{Organizational Alignment.} A future research direction concerned with how module structure interacts with team organization, ownership models following Conway's Law, DevOps maturity, and developer onboarding.

  \item \textbf{Guideline Orientation.} A future research direction concerned with how guidance itself should be packaged, including actionable patterns, context-sensitive recommendations, and explicit dilemmas rather than rigid rules.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
Each dimension group provides a set of guidelines that, together, form the foundation for the proposal of this research. It illustrates how technical, operational, organizational, and orientation-based considerations should be integrated when selecting or evolving a modular monolith architecture in software startups. What follows is a descriptive presentation of the guidelines within each dimension and their foundation concepts, starting with those that address the architectural design of modular monoliths.

\section{Future Research}
\label{sec:future-guidelines}

This dissertation proposes six guidelines (G1--G6) that operationalize the Architectural Design and Operational Fit dimensions. The remaining two dimensions, Organizational Alignment and Guideline Orientation, are framed as future research because they require empirical methods beyond code-level analysis, including organizational case studies, practitioner surveys, and longitudinal team observations. The open questions in each dimension are summarized below as a research agenda rather than as pre-committed guidelines.

\subsection*{Organizational Alignment}

Open questions in this dimension include how module boundaries should map to team boundaries following Conway's Law, including code ownership policies, module stewardship, and the organizational cost of cross-module changes; what DevOps practices are required to sustain a modular monolith in production, including deployment frequency, mean time to recovery, and infrastructure-as-code adoption; and how architectural decisions affect the time and effort required for new team members to become productive, including documentation quality, local development setup, and module discoverability.

\subsection*{Guideline Orientation}

Open questions in this dimension include how to package architectural guidance as reusable templates and code references that reduce ambiguity when applying the guidelines in practice; how to adapt recommendations to the specific business context, team size, and growth stage of the organization; and how to frame guidance around the trade-offs and dilemmas inherent in architectural decisions rather than as prescriptive rules.

\section{Verification Plan}
\label{sec:verification-plan}

To strengthen the practical and academic relevance of the architectural guidelines proposed in this chapter, a two-phase verification is planned. The first phase targets the six guidelines (G1--G6) through semi-structured interviews with industry practitioners. Future phases addressing the open research directions identified in Section~\ref{sec:future-guidelines} will adopt the same interview-based methodology, complemented by organizational case studies as appropriate to the dimension under study.

Participants will include software engineering practitioners from industry and researchers from academia, providing both practice and research perspectives. All participants will sign an informed-consent form covering the study's purpose, data use, and right to withdraw. Recordings will be stored securely with restricted access, and transcripts will be anonymized before analysis, following standard research ethics practice.

\subsection*{Interview Method}

The primary verification method consists of semi-structured interviews with selected practitioners. Participants will include professionals with relevant expertise in designing or evolving monolithic and microservices systems or leading software architectural decisions in startups. Each interview will explore the perceived usefulness, feasibility and completeness of the guidelines as well as any limitations or omissions experts may identify. Feedback will be used to refine the guidelines.

Interviews will be conducted remotely and will follow a flexible script structured around four thematic areas:

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item General impressions and relevance of the guideline set
  \item Detailed feedback on the wording, examples, and exercises
  \item Contextual challenges in applying the guidelines in early-stage, cloud-native applications
  \item Suggestions for improving or decomposing guidelines into more actionable steps
\end{enumerate}

Participants will receive summarized descriptions of the guidelines in advance to facilitate discussion. With participant consent, all interviews will be recorded and transcribed. Transcripts will be stored securely and later coded using thematic analysis.

\subsection*{Analysis Approach}

Interview transcripts will be examined to identify recurring themes. The researcher will verify:

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=8pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item Viability barriers (e.g., tooling limitations, organizational culture misalignment, lack of time or resources)
  \item Viability enablers (e.g., clear boundaries, simplicity of implementation, improved modularity)
  \item Gaps or missing details (e.g., unclear terminology, need for additional examples or prerequisites)
\end{enumerate}

Feedback will be categorized according to the four evaluation dimensions defined in Chapter~\ref{sec:relatedwork}. The researcher will cross-reference these comments against the original literature gaps identified in the systematic review to confirm that the guidelines address underexplored needs and to inform any refinements.
