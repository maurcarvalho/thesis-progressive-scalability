Cloud-native software startups face an architectural tension: microservices introduce premature operational complexity for small teams, while traditional monoliths degrade into tightly coupled systems that resist change. This research addresses the gap between these extremes by developing six architectural guidelines (G1--G6) for progressive scalability in modular monolith applications.

A systematic literature review of 18 peer-reviewed studies published between 2019 and 2024 identified twelve evaluation criteria across four dimensions and revealed that existing studies cover individual techniques in isolation, but none integrate them into a cohesive guideline set for progressive scalability. The contribution of this research is that integration: the G1$\rightarrow$G6 progression synthesizes individually studied techniques into a step-by-step scalability path. The six guidelines cover modular boundaries (G1), maintainability (G2), progressive scalability across a four-level spectrum (G3), migration readiness with Kafka and Temporal (G4), deployment strategy across a D0--D2 spectrum (G5), and observability with OpenTelemetry (G6). Together, they make module extraction a deployment-topology change rather than an architectural rewrite.

The guidelines are grounded in Tiny Store, a working reference implementation built as an Nx monorepo with four bounded contexts. Verification through semi-structured interviews with expert practitioners is planned as the next research phase.
